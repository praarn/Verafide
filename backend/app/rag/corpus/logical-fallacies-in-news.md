# Logical fallacies common in misleading news

## Correlation presented as causation

Two things happen near each other in time and the piece implies one caused
the other. Vaccination rates and an unrelated health trend, a policy and a
market move the same week. Ask whether a plausible mechanism is stated and
whether confounders were considered.

## Cherry-picking

A real but unrepresentative fact is highlighted while contradicting data is
omitted: one cold day used to dismiss a climate trend, one outlier study
against a large body of evidence. Check whether the cited fact is typical
or an exception.

## False balance

Giving two positions equal weight when the evidence is lopsided ("some
scientists disagree" when it is a fringe minority). Weight of evidence
matters, not just the existence of a dissenter.

## Ad hominem and guilt by association

Attacking the person or their associates instead of the claim. The
character of a messenger does not by itself make a factual claim true or
false.

## Strawman

Restating an opponent's position in an exaggerated form that is easier to
attack. Compare the quoted position to what the person actually said.
