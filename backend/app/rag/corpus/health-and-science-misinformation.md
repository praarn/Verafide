# Health and science misinformation

## Recurring patterns

- **Miracle cure / single cause:** one food, supplement, or device claimed
  to prevent or cure a wide range of conditions.
- **Suppression narrative:** "doctors/Big Pharma don't want you to know",
  used to pre-empt the absence of clinical evidence.
- **Anecdote as proof:** individual testimonials substituted for trials.
- **Chemical-name scaremongering:** treating any unfamiliar compound name
  as inherently toxic, ignoring dose.
- **Appeal to nature:** "natural" equated with safe, "synthetic" with
  harmful.
- **Preprint or retracted study cited as settled science.**

## Verification

Check whether the claim is supported by systematic reviews or guidance from
bodies like the WHO, CDC, NHS, or Cochrane. Look at study type
(randomized controlled trial > observational > animal > in vitro), sample
size, and whether it was peer reviewed. A claim that contradicts strong
scientific consensus needs correspondingly strong, replicated evidence —
not a single blog post or video.

## Framing to watch

Urgency ("share to save lives"), distrust of all institutions, and the
promise of secret knowledge are all signals that a health claim is being
sold rather than reported.
