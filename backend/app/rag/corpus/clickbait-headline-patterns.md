# Clickbait headline patterns

## Curiosity gap

The headline withholds the key fact to force a click: "You won't believe
what happened next", "This one trick", "The reason why will shock you". A
straight news headline states the news.

## Vagueness and pronoun teasing

"They finally admitted it", "This changes everything" — no subject, no
specifics. The reader cannot assess the claim without clicking, which is
the point.

## Overpromising

Superlatives that the body never supports: "DESTROYS", "SLAMS", "goes
viral", "breaks the internet". The verbs are doing work the facts cannot.

## Question headlines

"Is [alarming thing] happening?" lets the outlet imply a claim it cannot
substantiate; Betteridge's law notes the answer is usually "no".

## How to check

Read the body and see whether it delivers a specific, attributable fact
that matches the headline. A large gap between headline drama and body
substance is a strong signal of low-quality or manipulative content.
