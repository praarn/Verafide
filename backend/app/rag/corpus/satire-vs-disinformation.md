# Satire versus disinformation

## The difference

Satire uses a news format to make a point through humor and exaggeration
and does not intend to be believed as literal fact. Disinformation is
false content created to deceive. The same fabricated headline can start as
satire and become disinformation when it is stripped of context and
re-shared as real.

## Markers of satire

- A masthead known for satire (The Onion, The Babylon Bee, Waterford
  Whispers, Faking News).
- Absurd escalation: the premise starts plausible and becomes impossible.
- A punchline structure rather than an inverted-pyramid news structure.
- Bylines, "about" pages, or disclaimers identifying the outlet as satire.

## How context gets lost

A screenshot of a satirical headline with the source cropped out, shared
with an outraged caption, functions as disinformation for anyone who does
not recognize the outlet. Reverse-search the headline and identify the
original publisher before reacting.

## For an automated classifier

Style alone is ambiguous — measured satire can read as a real report. The
publishing domain is often the decisive signal, which is why a
source-reputation check belongs alongside a text model.
