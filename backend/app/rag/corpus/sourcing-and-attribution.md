# Sourcing and attribution

## The standard

Credible journalism tells you where information came from: named people,
named documents, named institutions, or — when a source must be protected
— a specific description of their position and why anonymity was granted.
Multiple independent sources are expected for contested claims.

## Weak-sourcing red flags

- "Sources say" or "experts believe" with no expert ever named.
- A single anonymous source for an extraordinary claim.
- Circular sourcing: outlet A cites outlet B, which cites outlet A.
- Quotes with no speaker, setting, or date.
- Screenshots of screenshots with no link to an original.
- "Studies show" without naming the study, journal, or authors.

## How to check

Trace each key claim to its origin. A real study has a DOI, a journal, and
authors with affiliations. A real official statement has a transcript, a
press release, or a spokesperson's name. If the trail ends at another
aggregator or a deleted social post, the claim is unverified regardless of
how many outlets repeated it.
