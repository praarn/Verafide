# Manipulated and miscaptioned images

## The most common visual falsehood is not a fake image

It is a real image with a false caption: a photo from a different year,
country, or event presented as current. This "miscontextualization" is
cheap, fast, and hard to spot without a reverse image search.

## Signs to check in the image itself

- Inconsistent lighting or shadows between subjects.
- Warped or smeared edges around inserted objects (classic splicing).
- Repeating textures or cloned patches (used to remove or duplicate
  elements).
- Text on signs or screens that is garbled or physically implausible.
- Impossible reflections, or a subject whose resolution differs sharply
  from the background.

## Verification workflow

1. Reverse image search (Google Lens, TinEye, Yandex) to find earlier
   appearances.
2. Check the earliest known publication date and original caption.
3. Look for landmarks, signage, license plates, or weather that fix the
   location and season.
4. For AI-generated images, look at hands, teeth, jewelry, background
   text, and fine repeating detail, which current generators still get
   wrong.
