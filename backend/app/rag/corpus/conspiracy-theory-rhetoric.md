# Conspiracy theory rhetoric

## Structural tells

- **Unfalsifiability:** absence of evidence is recast as proof of a
  cover-up ("that's exactly what they'd want you to think").
- **Connecting unrelated dots:** disparate events are woven into a single
  intentional plan, with coincidence treated as impossible.
- **A vague, all-powerful "they":** an actor with unlimited competence and
  reach, yet somehow foiled by an anonymous blog post.
- **Just asking questions:** implying a claim without stating it, so it
  cannot be fact-checked directly.
- **Persecuted-insider framing:** the author alone has courage to speak;
  every institution and expert is compromised.

## Why the style matters

Conspiracy content often contains no checkable factual assertion at all —
it is an interpretive frame laid over real events. A text classifier
should weight the rhetorical structure (rhetorical questions, appeals to
hidden knowledge, blanket distrust of sourcing) rather than looking for a
single false fact.

## How to engage

Ask what specific, dated, attributable evidence would change the author's
mind. If the answer is "nothing could", it is a belief system, not a
report.
